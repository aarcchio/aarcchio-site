2023 BRZ ZD8


Street Built Performance


Following the philosophy of the original AE86 chassis which focused on delivering a pure driving experience over raw engine power, my 2022 BRZ build places a focus on improving the handling and feel of the car to maximise its back road performance. With a minimal mod list, the build looks to preserve the original oem characteristics of the car, predominantly changing hard limitations from the factory to improve its handling capabilities
With upgrades targeting the car’s suspension, modifications include a much wider set of Forged TE 37SLs s pecced at 18x9.5 + 40 over the stock 18x7.5 + 48 with supporting ST c oilovers to allow the extra wheel to clear the inner wheel well. Cusco Rear Upper Control arms were also installed to allow for camber adjustment in the rear which the original suspension couldn’t accommodate and camber bolts in the front for a more aggressive stance.

Over my course of ownership I’ve gone through three different tire sizes including 255/35/18, 245/40/18 and 245/35/18. At one end of the spectrum the 255/35 wide tires gave the car a lower and wider stance at the expense of my sanity with rubbing being an issue to the sheer size of the rubber whilst on the other end the 245/40 gave the car a much more comfier and enjoyable ride at the expense of increasing the wheel’s radius and subsequently lifting the car by around half an inch. This also caused some rubbing at full lock on the chassis due to the bigger wheel dimensions which prompted me to switch back to a 35mm tall sidewall.

Visually, the car has a minimal amount of work done to it with the only changes being back and side window tint to help keep the car cool during the Melbourne Summer and a ‘high kick’ rear spoiler to give the rear more prominence, building on the car’s already visually stunning body lines. This change also gives the cars side profile a more aggressive look to it with the high kick eccentuating the shape of the rear.
