BIG Conditions

Tooronga 3146 Precinct



Activity Centre Alternative Development


How should future developments be considered within Melbourne? big conditions looks to explore the Victorian Government’s ‘Activity Centre’ inititative, speculating how density could start to form in low density suburbs. The project explores how developments can be used as a means to restore urban conditions and mediate between built outcomes and retaining the character and programs of existing space.

Big conditions speculates on a place specific proposal that integrates the existing landscape and ground conditions of Tooronga and repositions challenging or inconvenient characteristics of the space into opportunities for new programs. Through creating adaptive and living ground conditions that can react to the ever changing needs of its population, big conditions approaches site through an infrastructural lens creating sustainable and scalable systems that evolve with the suburbs growth.

Through assessing how the site can be developed as a whole opposed to conventional individual developments, big conditions explores an alternative master planning strategy that considers landscape, ecology and urban insertions as one unified system that negotiates with each other challenging the current individualistic developer led model.

Project Drawings

Site Forces Diagram


These force diagrams investigate the environmental and spatial conditions that define the site, including circulation, acoustics, wind, topography, visibility and existing edges. Each force informs a series of interface typologies that respond through changes in permeability, orientation and spatial organisation. Rather than designing against the site, the project uses these conditions as generators of architectural form, allowing new relationships between landscape, infrastructure and architecture to emerge.

Site Forces Diagram


The landscape strategy is organised through the interaction of circulation, topography, hydrology and program. Drawing on Jan Gehl's distinction between necessary and optional activities, communal spaces are located alongside primary movement corridors, recognising that social life emerges where people already gather. Hydrological systems are embedded within the landscape through swales, floodable open space and vegetation, while programs respond to the site's changing environmental conditions. Rather than prescribing a fixed outcome, the strategy establishes a framework that can continue to evolve as future needs emerge.

Interface Taxonomy Table


While the landscape establishes the broader infrastructural framework of the project, the architecture responds to these same environmental forces at the scale of the interface. Rather than representing a fixed catalogue of solutions, the interface studies explore how different combinations of site conditions, activities and topographical strata can generate diverse architectural responses. Together, they demonstrate how environmental forces can inform building form, spatial organisation and the relationships that emerge between architecture, landscape and public space.

These images depict how the landscape adapts to changing hydrological conditions, shifting between civic space and flood infrastructure over time. Rather than resisting water, the landscape works alongside it, integrating flood infrastructure into the public realm to reclaim land previously considered unsuitable for development due to flooding, while allowing environmental processes to shape how the site is experienced and understood.
